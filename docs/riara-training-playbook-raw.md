# Riara University Design Thinking Sprint: Training Workshop Playbook

*Verbatim documentation transcribed directly from the pre-hackathon training sessions led by Stephen Kagwathi (Riara University Social Entrepreneurship & Innovation Hub - SEIH).*

---

## Session 1: "Don't Solve It Yet. Find The Problem Worth Solving"
**Date:** Wednesday, October 7, 2026 | **Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Theme:** EdTech Innovation + Cybersecurity  
**Output:** One clear Problem Statement + one focused How Might We (HMW) question.

### 1. The Design Thinking Mindset
> *"A human-centered approach to understanding needs, creating ideas, and learning through testing."*

The core process spans four foundational stages:
```
1. UNDERSTAND        2. DEFINE               3. CREATE               4. TEST
(People, needs,      (Insight, focused       (Ideas,                 (Prototype, feedback,
 context)             problem)                possibilities)          learning)
```
- **The Non-Linear Reality**: The process is not a straight line. You may learn something during testing or prototyping that sends you back to re-define the problem.
- **The Engine**: `LEARN -> CHANGE -> LEARN AGAIN`.

---

### 2. Empathize: Look Before You Solve
> *"The goal is not to collect opinions. It is to understand the person's experience."*

Four Observation Quadrants:
1. **OBSERVE**: What is happening in their daily environment?
2. **ASK**: What is the person actively trying to do?
3. **LISTEN**: What frustrates, worries, or motivates them?
4. **NOTICE**: What workarounds or alternatives do they use instead?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Fieldwork Golden Rule**: In the real hackathon, talk to users whenever possible. Do not design entirely from inside your team's heads.

---

### 3. Define: Turn a Topic Into a Problem
> *"A topic is not yet a problem you can design for."*

To turn a vague topic into a designable challenge, continuously interrogate:  
**Ask:** *WHO? WHERE? WHEN? WHAT PAIN? WHY DOES IT MATTER?*

#### Progression Example:
- **Too Broad**: *"Cybersecurity in universities."*
- **Better**: *"Students struggle with cybersecurity."*
- **Designable**: *"First-year students struggle to recognise suspicious links sent through university communication channels."*

---

### 4. Build Your Problem Card
Every team must exit Session 1 with their Problem Card completed:

| Component | Guiding Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| **USER** | Who are we designing for? |
| **CONTEXT** | When and where does the problem happen? |
| **PAIN** | What is difficult, frustrating, or risky? |
| **INSIGHT** | What have we learned that actually matters? |
| **PROBLEM STATEMENT** | **USER + NEED + CONTEXT + WHY IT MATTERS** |
| **HOW MIGHT WE...?** | Turn the problem into an open invitation to create. |

---

### 5. Crafting the "How Might We" (HMW) Question
Good HMW questions are focused enough to guide the team, yet open enough to unlock creative possibilities:

- **Too Narrow**: *"How might we build a two-factor authentication app for first-years?"* (Designs the solution into the question).
- **Too Broad**: *"How might we improve cybersecurity?"* (Provides zero actionable direction).
- **Just Right**: *"How might we help first-year students recognise and respond to suspicious university messages without making digital learning harder?"*

> [!WARNING]
> **Anti-Pattern**: Avoid designing the solution into the question.

## Session 2: "Now Build To Learn"
**Date:** Wednesday, October 14, 2026 | **Duration:** 60 Minutes  
**Output:** One promising concept + a rough prototype + feedback + one improvement.

### 1. Choose With a Hackathon Filter
> *"Do not choose the coolest idea. Choose the strongest opportunity."*

Evaluate and score every concept (1 to 5) across the **4 Pillars**:
1. **DESIRABLE**: Does someone actually need it?
2. **USEFUL**: Does it address our defined problem directly?
3. **FEASIBLE**: Can we build and demonstrate it in 48 hours?
4. **TRUSTWORTHY**: Does it respect privacy, security, and user safety?

---

### 2. Prototype: Make It Visible
> *"Do not build the final product. Build enough for someone to react to it."*

#### The 6 Prototyping Archetypes:
1. **Sketch**: A rough drawing of the concept.
2. **Storyboard**: The visual user journey across time.
3. **Screen Mock-up**: Key screen layouts and content hierarchy.
4. **Role Play**: Acting out the operational or human experience.
5. **Clickable**: A simple interactive UI click-through.
6. **Demo**: A thin, functional working slice.

> [!TIP]
> **The Prototyping Law**: Choose the cheapest prototype that can answer your biggest question:  
> **`One Question -> One Prototype`**.

---

### 3. The 80/20 User Testing Rule
When putting the prototype in front of a user or judge:
- **80% User Talking / 20% Team Talking**: Let the user speak for 80% of the session. If you must speak, keep it to 20%.
- **Observe Non-Verbal Signals**: Watch body language, hesitations, where they click twice, and signs of excitement or confusion.
- **Do Not Defend or Sell**: The biggest mistake is trying to "sell" the prototype because you fell in love with it. Let users criticize the product ruthlessly.
- **Focus on the Excluded**: Pay close attention to users who currently don't use digital tools or struggle with technical access.

---

### 4. The 48-Hour Design Thinking Competition Playbook
Take this exact 8-step sequence into presentation day:

```
01 UNDERSTAND  --> Who are we solving for?
02 DEFINE      --> What problem are we actually solving?
03 IDEATE      --> What could we do differently?
04 CHOOSE      --> Which direction gives the most value?
05 PROTOTYPE   --> What is the fastest way to make it tangible?
06 TEST        --> What does the user say?
07 IMPROVE     --> What should we change based on feedback?
08 PITCH       --> Can we explain the problem, evidence, solution & value?
```

### 5. Pitching Requirements Before the Judges
1. **Third-Party Evidence**: Cite verified articles, national audits, or statistics proving the problem exists in the real world.
2. **Differentiation & Value**: Show the tangible delta between current workarounds and your solution.
3. **Live Tangible Prototype**: Pitch with a working slice, not abstract slides.
4. **Documented User Feedback**: Present exact quotes from testing and show what you improved.
